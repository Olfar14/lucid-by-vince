Stoic Provocation · May 2026
"The impediment to action advances action. What stands in the way becomes the way."
Marcus Aurelius, Meditations V.20
A US-Iran war that cratered markets for three months became the ceasefire that launched them. The obstacle — geopolitical chaos — became the catalyst for the best equity run of the year.
Question of the Month
When markets reach all-time highs while consumer sentiment hits record lows — who is right?
May 2026 forces this question sharply: S&P at 7,580 ATH, UMich sentiment at 44.8 (record low). Markets price the future; sentiment measures the present. One of them is badly wrong — or both are, for different reasons. Keynes called this the paradox of thrift. Kahneman would call it two systems running out of sync.
Cognitive Bias of the Month
Apophenia — seeing patterns where none exist
After every geopolitical shock, analysts construct inevitability narratives. The ceasefire "obviously" ended oil's bull run. In hindsight, everything looks causal. In the moment, it was noise. Apophenia is the tendency to find meaningful connections in random events — the bias that makes markets predictable only in retrospect, never in advance.

Bitcoin slid to ~$73,675 late May. Ethereum held ~$2,200-$2,345. Risk appetite rotated from crypto toward equities.

Word You Should Know
May 2026
Imaginal
Adjective · from Latin imaginalis
Relating to the realm of the imagination as a distinct cognitive space — used in depth psychology (Hillman, Corbin) to describe images that are neither purely literal nor purely abstract, but carry their own reality. In a month where market "reality" and consumer "reality" violently diverged, imaginal thinking — holding two contradictory truths simultaneously — may be the most useful cognitive skill available. The market is not wrong. The consumer is not wrong. They are imagining different futures.
